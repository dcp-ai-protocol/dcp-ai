## 1. Scope and Disclaimer

DCP-AI is a protocol and an evidence format. It defines signed artifacts (in a bundle, the Responsible Principal Record, Agent Passport, Intent and Policy Decision are each a `SignedPayload`; lifecycle, dispute, rights and delegation records are signed by their own functions), audit entries that a signed bundle commits to through its audit Merkle root instead of signing them one by one, a tamper-evident audit trail, optional anchoring and transparency logging, and local verifiers, each of which checks only what its route covers (for example `verifySignedBundleV2` for a V2 bundle).

DCP-AI is **not**:

- a conformity assessment, a harmonised standard, a common specification or a presumption of conformity under the AI Act;
- a risk management system (Art. 9), a quality management system, technical documentation (Art. 11) or a post-market monitoring plan (Art. 72) for any particular AI system;
- legal advice. Whether a given agent is an "AI system", whether it is high-risk, and who is the provider or deployer, is a determination the organisation must make with counsel.

What this document does is narrower: for each obligation listed, it names the DCP mechanism that produces evidence or enforces a control, says how far that mechanism goes (`supports`, `partial`, `out of scope`) and states what the provider or deployer must still do themselves. A row marked `supports` means the mechanism exists and does what the row says when configured as described in Section 5; it does not mean the obligation is discharged.

Coverage values used in Section 3:

| Value | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| `supports` | A DCP artifact, verifier rule or reference service implements the mechanism the obligation calls for. Operating it correctly remains the organisation's job (Section 5). |
| `partial` | DCP produces evidence or a building block, but a material part of the obligation (a document, a process, a report, a human decision) is not something the protocol can produce. |
| `out of scope` | DCP does not address this and should not be cited for it. |

## 2. Roles: Provider and Deployer versus Operator, Responsible Principal and Agent

The AI Act attaches obligations to roles defined in [Art. 3](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689#art_3). A **provider** (Art. 3(3)) develops an AI system, or has it developed, and places it on the market or puts it into service under its own name. A **deployer** (Art. 3(4)) uses an AI system "under its authority", except in a personal non-professional activity. An **AI system** (Art. 3(1)) is a machine-based system operating with "varying levels of autonomy" that infers how to generate outputs from the input it receives. The Act's obligations attach to the AI system and the legal persons behind it.

DCP roles attach to an agent instance and to the human or organisation that answers for it:

| DCP role | Where it is recorded | Typical AI Act role | Notes |
|----------|---------------------|---------------------|-------|
| **Responsible principal** | `schemas/v2/responsible_principal_record.schema.json`: `human_id`, `legal_name`, `entity_type` (`natural_person` or `organization`), `jurisdiction`, `liability_mode` (`owner_responsible`), `override_rights`, `binding_keys` | **Deployer**, or the natural person the deployer assigns human oversight to under Art. 26(2) | The RPR names who is responsible for the agent's actions. When `entity_type` is `organization` it is usually the deployer itself; when it is `natural_person` it is usually the person inside the deployer who holds `override_rights`. |
| **Agent** | `schemas/v2/agent_passport.schema.json`: `agent_id`, `keys`, `principal_binding_reference`, `capabilities`, `risk_tier`, `status` | The **AI system** (or a component of one) | A DCP agent is one deployed instance with its own keys and lifecycle state. The provider of the underlying model or framework is not named in any DCP artifact. |
| **Operator** (of a verification service, policy engine, transparency log, anchor or revocation service) | `server/`, `services/`, verifier policy ([OPERATOR_GUIDE.md](OPERATOR_GUIDE.md)) | Usually the **deployer**; may be the **provider** when the provider ships the gating as part of the system, or a third party | The operator decides the verifier policy, the retention of audit data and the anchoring cadence. Several Section 5 items are the operator's. |
| **Counterparty agent** (DCP-04, DCP-09) | `interaction_record.counterparty_agent_id`, delegation mandates | Another AI system, possibly another deployer | Interaction records give each side evidence of the other's mandate. |

Two consequences for the mapping:

1. **Provider-side obligations are mostly `partial`.** DCP does not know the model, the training data or the intended purpose. It can document how the accountability layer works (Section 3, Art. 11 row), not the AI system itself.
2. **Deployer-side logging and oversight obligations are where DCP is strongest.** Intent gating, policy decisions, the audit chain, the RPR's `override_rights`, advisory declarations and lifecycle states are all deployer-facing controls that produce signed evidence.

The DCP-AI specifications use "human" and "principal" for the person bound by the RPR. This document uses "responsible principal" for that person and "deployer" and "provider" in the Act's sense.

| [Art. 73(6)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689#art_73) second subparagraph | After a serious incident, the provider must not alter the AI system in a way that affects the later evaluation of the incident's causes before informing the authorities. | Anchoring (`services/anchor`, `anchor_receipt` in [STORAGE_AND_ANCHORING.md](STORAGE_AND_ANCHORING.md)) and the transparency log (`services/transparency-log`: `POST /add`, `GET /proof/:index`, `GET /root/signed`, RFC 6962 tree, Ed25519-signed tree heads) record a commitment to the state of the audit trail, so a later evaluator can tell whether the retained log matches what was committed ([SECURITY_MODEL.md](SECURITY_MODEL.md) §6, "Retroactive modification"). ### 3.2 Human oversight

| Article | Obligation (paraphrase) | DCP mechanism | Coverage | What remains for the organisation |
|---------|------------------------|---------------|----------|-----------------------------------|
| [Art. 14(1)–(3)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689#art_14) | High-risk systems must be designed so that natural persons can effectively oversee them while in use; measures are commensurate with risk, autonomy and context, built in by the provider or implemented by the deployer. | **Intent gating** (DCP-02): no action without a declared Intent and a Policy Decision (`approve`, `escalate`, `block`); `required_confirmation` requests a Human Confirmation before the action. **Adaptive scrutiny**: the security tier (core §14, `computeSecurityTier`, `effectiveVerificationMode`) rises with `risk_score`, `data_classes` (`pii`, `financial_data`, `health_data`, `credentials`, `children_data`), `action_type: "initiate_payment"` and `estimated_impact: "high"`, and a verifier MUST NOT downgrade it (core §14.3). **Lifecycle states** (DCP-05 §3): `commissioned`, `active`, `declining`, `decommissioned`; the specification requires principal approval to reactivate from `declining`, but `validateStateTransition` only checks that the transition is one of the allowed edges, not the approval. | `partial` | Decide which actions are gated and which policy produces `escalate`. The application or its integration enforces the gate on what is declared, and verifiers check the resulting evidence; the provider must make the AI system call the gate. The oversight *measures* (who, how, with what training) are the deployer's under Art. 26(2). |
| [Art. 14(4)(a)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689#art_14) | Overseers must be able to understand the system's capacities and limitations and monitor its operation, including detecting anomalies and unexpected performance. | **Awareness thresholds** (DCP-09 §4, `schemas/v2/awareness_threshold.schema.json`): signed `threshold_rules[]` with `dimension`, `operator`, `value` and `action_if_triggered` (`notify`, `escalate`, `block`); `createAwarenessThreshold`, `evaluateSignificance`, `shouldNotifyHuman`. **Principal mirror** (DCP-09 §6, `schemas/v2/principal_mirror.schema.json`, `generateMirror`). **Vitality reports** (DCP-05 §5–6, `createVitalityReport`) with anomaly detection (§6.5). **Observability** ([OBSERVABILITY.md](OBSERVABILITY.md), `dcpTelemetry`): verify latency, bundles verified, errors and checkpoint latency by tier. | `partial` | The capacities and limitations of the AI system itself (accuracy, robustness, known failure modes) come from the provider's instructions for use (Art. 13). DCP tells the overseer what the agent *did* and what it *declared it does not understand*, not what the model can do. |
| [Art. 14(4)(b)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689#art_14) | Overseers must remain aware of automation bias. | None. | `out of scope` | Training and interface design. The narrative layer of interaction records (row below) can make reasoning visible, which helps, but DCP does not address automation bias. |
| [Art. 14(4)(c)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689#art_14) | Overseers must be able to interpret the system's output correctly. | **Interaction records** (DCP-09 §7, `schemas/v2/interaction_record.schema.json`): a human-readable `public_layer` (`terms`, `decisions`, `commitments`) and a `private_layer_hash` committing to the technical detail; `generateInteractionRecord`. DCP explains the agent's declared intent and its reasoning as recorded, not the model's output. |
| [Art. 14(4)(d)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689#art_14) | Overseers must be able to decide not to use the system or to disregard, override or reverse its output. | **`override_rights`** (boolean, required) in the RPR and copied unchanged into the Blinded RPR (core §12.1): the responsible principal's right to override is a signed, published attribute of the binding. **Human Confirmation** `decision: "deny"` on an escalated intent. **Advisory response** (DCP-09 §5.5): proceed, override, assume control, delegate elsewhere or acknowledge, signed by the principal and recorded in the audit chain; `advisory_declaration.human_response`, `response_deadline`, `proceeded_without_response`. **Awareness rule** `action_if_triggered: "block"`. | `supports` | Set `override_rights: true` for every agent used in a high-risk context and make the application or its integration enforce a `deny` and a `block`: verifiers do not execute either decision or confirm that a human decided, and neither verification nor admission authorizes an action (an admission result carries `action_authorized: false`). Define who the principal is and keep that person's keys under their control (Section 5). |
| [Art. 14(4)(e)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689#art_14) | Overseers must be able to intervene or interrupt the system through a "stop" button or similar, bringing it to a safe state. | **Emergency revocation** (core §11.3, `schemas/v2/emergency_revocation.schema.json`): the pre-registered `emergency_revocation_token` in the passport is `SHA-256(revocation_secret)`; revealing the secret revokes all of the agent's keys with no signature needed (`generateEmergencyRevocationToken`, `buildEmergencyRevocation`, `verifyEmergencyRevocationSecret`; `POST /v2/emergency-revoke` on the revocation service and the gateway, `GET /v2/check/kid/:kid` on the revocation service; the signed kid-level revocation is the revocation service's `POST /v2/revoke`). **Revocation record** (`schemas/v2/revocation_record.schema.json`, `reason` enum includes `key_compromise_emergency`). **Lifecycle** transition to `declining` or `decommissioned` (DCP-05 §3.2, `validateStateTransition`, `createDecommissioningRecord`), `termination_for_cause` mode (§7.3). Verifiers MUST invalidate cached results when a signer kid is revoked (core §14.5). | `partial` | Revocation stops *acceptance* of the agent's bundles by every verifier that checks revocations; it does not halt a running process or roll back an action already taken. The safe-state behaviour of the AI system is the provider's design. Pair revocation with an application-level kill switch, and meet the revocation propagation latency for the tier ([SECURITY_MODEL.md](SECURITY_MODEL.md), "Revocation Propagation SLAs"). |
| [Art. 14(5)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689#art_14) | For remote biometric identification (Annex III, point 1(a)): no action on an identification unless verified by at least two competent natural persons (with exceptions for law enforcement, migration, border control, asylum). | **Multi-party authorization** (`schemas/v2/multi_party_authorization.schema.json`, gateway `POST /v2/multi-party/authorize`, each party's signature verified against its registered keys and counted once). | `partial` | DCP can evidence that two distinct key holders approved an intent; it cannot evidence their competence or that the identification was "separately verified". The two-person rule is the deployer's process. |
| [Art. 26(1)–(2)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689#art_26) | Deployers use the system in line with the instructions for use and assign human oversight to natural persons with the necessary competence, training, authority and support. | The RPR binds the agent to a named natural person or organisation with `override_rights`; the Delegation Mandate (DCP-09 §3, `schemas/v2/delegation_mandate.schema.json`, `createDelegationMandate`, `verifyMandateValidity`, `revokeDelegation`) states what the agent may do on the principal's behalf (`authority_scope`: `domain`, `actions_permitted`, `data_classes`, `limits`; the prohibitions list of the DCP-09 §3.2 example is not a schema field); `interaction_record` and DCP-09 §7.5 record delegation compliance. | `partial` | Competence, training and support are organisational facts outside the record. Name the oversight person in the RPR (`entity_type: "natural_person"`) or keep an internal register that maps an `organization` RPR to the persons assigned. |
| [Art. 26(5)](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/HTML/?uri=CELEX:32024R1689#art_26) | Deployers monitor the system's operation, inform the provider under Art. 72, and if use may present a risk, inform the provider or distributor and the market surveillance authority and suspend use; serious incidents are reported first to the provider. | **Evidence of operation**: audit chain, Policy Decisions, interaction records, obligation records with `compliance_status` (DCP-08 §5, `recordObligation`, `checkRightsCompliance`), vitality reports. **Suspension**: passport `status` (`suspended`, `declining`, `decommissioned`), revocation, emergency revocation. **Escalation**: DCP-08 §6.4 specifies that an obligation breach opens a DCP-07 dispute, with the escalation deadlines of DCP-07 §3.2; the SDK does not open it automatically (`reportViolation` takes a `dispute_id` that the caller supplies, or `null`). | `partial` | Informing the provider, the distributor and the market surveillance authority, and deciding that a risk exists, are the deployer's acts. DCP has no artifact for a report to an authority. |

### 3.4 Explanation, redress and disputes

## 5. Implementer Checklist

What a deployer or operator must configure or operate for the `supports` rows of Section 3 to be true in their deployment. Items marked (provider) are for a provider that ships DCP gating inside the AI system.

### 5.1 Logging and retention (Arts. 12, 19, 26(6))

- [ ] Every consequential action of the AI system is declared as an Intent and gated by a Policy Decision before execution; actions that bypass gating are enumerated and justified in the instructions for use (provider).
- [ ] Audit entries are produced with the dual-hash chain (`hash_alg: "sha256+sha3-256"`, `prev_hash_secondary`) and PQ checkpoints at the interval of the resolved tier (core §14.1: every 50, 10 or 1 events).
- [ ] Signed bundles and audit entries (not only hashes) are written to durable storage under the organisation's control; the reference gateway, anchor, transparency log and revocation service hold state in memory and are not a store of record ([OPERATOR_GUIDE.md](OPERATOR_GUIDE.md), "Reference services keep state in memory only"). Set `REVOCATION_STATE_PATH` for the revocation service and add persistent storage before relying on the others.
- [ ] A retention period of at least six months is set for those records, extended where the intended purpose, sector law (e.g. financial services) or an ongoing incident requires it, and shortened only where data protection law requires it.
- [ ] Compaction checkpoints are used only with an archive of the compacted events for the retention period.
- [ ] The policy text or rules behind every `applied_policy_hash` are version-controlled and retained for the same period.
- [ ] Agents' public keys and the governance key set are archived at decommissioning (DCP-05 §8.4) so that retained signatures remain verifiable.
- [ ] Retained bundles are re-verified periodically with `verifySignedBundleV2` (or `dcp verify-bundle`) against the archived keys and revocation state, and the result is recorded.

### 5.2 Tamper evidence and anchoring (Art. 73(6), evidence for Arts. 72–73)

- [ ] Audit Merkle roots (or bundle hashes) are submitted to a transparency log and/or anchored at a fixed cadence. Choose a cadence shorter than the shortest incident reporting window that applies (two days under Art. 73(3)); hourly or daily batching is typical ([TECHNICAL_ARCHITECTURE.md](TECHNICAL_ARCHITECTURE.md), Layer 4).
- [ ] The transparency log runs with a persistent `OPERATOR_KEY` (so signed tree heads keep verifying across restarts), persistent storage, and at least one gossip peer with a pinned key (`GOSSIP_PEERS`).
- [ ] Inclusion proofs and anchor receipts (`anchor_receipt`: chain, transaction, block, or `log_url` and `log_index`) are stored next to the bundles they cover.
- [ ] If on-chain anchoring is required, a production anchor is deployed against `contracts/ethereum/src/DCPAnchor.sol` or a Bitcoin OP_RETURN flow; the reference anchor service alone records `tx_hash: null`.

### 5.3 Human oversight (Arts. 14, 26(1)–(2))

- [ ] Each agent in a high-risk context has an RPR with `override_rights: true`, a current `expires_at`, and `binding_keys` held by the responsible principal, not by the agent's runtime.
- [ ] The responsible principal (or the persons an `organization` RPR maps to) are the natural persons assigned to human oversight under Art. 26(2), and this mapping is kept in an internal register.
- [ ] Policy rules return `escalate` with `required_confirmation` for the action types, data classes and impact levels the deployer wants a human to approve; the application blocks on a missing or `deny` Human Confirmation.
- [ ] Awareness thresholds are declared per mandate with `action_if_triggered` set to `escalate` or `block` for the dimensions that matter; advisory declarations carry a `response_deadline` consistent with DCP-09 §5.3, and `proceeded_without_response` is monitored.
- [ ] An emergency revocation secret exists for every agent, is held by the responsible principal outside the agent's runtime, and the application has a kill switch that halts the process when revocation is triggered (revocation alone does not stop a running process).
- [ ] Verifiers check revocation state within the propagation latency of the highest tier served ([SECURITY_MODEL.md](SECURITY_MODEL.md), "Revocation Propagation SLAs"), and the verification cache TTL follows core §14.5. The TypeScript `VerificationCache` is keyed only by the bundle hash and its `invalidateByAgent` does nothing, so clear it when revocation or trust changes ([API_REFERENCE.md](API_REFERENCE.md)).
- [ ] For remote biometric identification, the two-person rule of Art. 14(5) is implemented with multi-party authorization over the intent and documented as a deployer process.

### 5.4 Key custody and verifier policy

- [ ] Private keys are stored per the tier requirements in [SECURITY_MODEL.md](SECURITY_MODEL.md) ("Private Key Storage"): HSM or TPM SHOULD for `elevated`, MUST for `maximum`. This repository provides no HSM or TPM integration (the `HsmCryptoProvider` in the TypeScript source is a simulation), so that custody comes from the organisation's own infrastructure.
- [ ] The verifier policy (`VerifierPolicy`, `VERIFIER_MODE`) uses at least `hybrid_preferred`, with `risk_overrides` that never fall below the tier's mode; `ALLOW_V1_BUNDLES=false` once migration is complete.
- [ ] Operator endpoints are locked with `OPERATOR_TOKEN`; `TRUST_PROXY` is set only behind a proxy that appends the client IP.
- [ ] The reference gateway's DCP-05–09 endpoints, which `OPERATOR_TOKEN` does not protect and which do not verify signatures, are not exposed to untrusted clients ([OPERATOR_GUIDE.md](OPERATOR_GUIDE.md#dcp-0509-service-endpoints)); records they hold are not treated as verified evidence.

### 5.5 Data protection (Section 3.7)

- [ ] RPRs are created with `pii_salt`; anchors and transparency logs receive only hashes, and counterparties outside the organisation see only Blinded RPRs.
- [ ] `human_id` is an opaque identifier, not an email, name or hash of either.
- [ ] A deletion procedure covers every copy of the full RPR, including copies disclosed to regulators or counterparties, so that erasure of the commitment is real.
- [ ] The data protection impact assessment (and, where Art. 27 applies, the fundamental rights impact assessment) lists the DCP records that contain `human_id` and their retention.

### 5.6 Documentation (Arts. 11, 13) — provider

- [ ] The instructions for use state which oversight measures are built into the system (Art. 14(3)(a)) and which the deployer must operate (Art. 14(3)(b)), and describe the log format and verification mechanism (Art. 13(3)(f)) by reference to pinned versions of this document, `spec/DCP-AI-v2.0.md`, `spec/VERIFICATION.md` and `schemas/v2/`.
- [ ] The Annex IV technical documentation cites DCP only for the accountability layer (points 1(b), 2(c), 2(h), 3) and names the DCP specification version, SDK package versions and the verifier policy in use.

## 6. Gaps: What DCP Does Not Address

These are stated so that nobody cites DCP for them.

| Area | Why DCP does not cover it |
|------|---------------------------|
| **Classification as high-risk, provider/deployer determination** | Legal determinations about the AI system and the organisations; DCP has no view of the system's intended purpose. |
| **Risk management system (Art. 9), quality management, conformity assessment, CE marking, EU database registration (Arts. 49, 71)** | Provider processes and administrative acts. `risk_score` is a per-action gating signal, not a risk assessment. |
| **Technical documentation of the AI system (Art. 11, Annex IV points 1(a), 1(c)–(h), 2(a)–(b), 2(d)–(g), 4–9)** | Describes the model, data, validation and changes; DCP documents only the accountability layer. |
| **Instructions for use (Art. 13(2)–(3))** | A provider document. DCP can be referenced in it, not substitute for it. |
| **Automation bias, competence and training of overseers (Arts. 14(4)(b), 26(2))** | Human factors; outside any record DCP produces. |
| **Halting the AI system in a safe state (Art. 14(4)(e))** | Revocation stops acceptance of the agent's bundles; it does not stop a process or undo an action. |
| **Biometric-specific logging (Art. 12(3)(a)–(c)) and the two-person verification itself (Art. 14(5))** | DCP has no fields for reference databases or matched inputs, and cannot attest competence. |
| **Content marking and deep-fake disclosure (Art. 50(2), 50(4))** | Separate technical measures on generated content. |
| **Disclosure to natural persons (Arts. 26(11), 50(1), 50(5))** | Interface and wording are the provider's and the deployer's; DCP supplies a verifiable proof of agent identity only. |
| **Reports to market surveillance authorities and to the provider (Arts. 26(5), 73)** | DCP has no report artifact. `rights_violation_report` concerns the agent's DCP-08 rights (memory integrity, dignified transition, identity consistency, immutable record); `dispute_record` concerns conflicts between agents. Neither is a serious-incident report. |
| **Complaints and explanations for affected natural persons (Arts. 27(1)(f), 85, 86)** | DCP-07 disputes and DCP-08 rights are mechanisms between agents and their principals. Affected persons are not parties in the protocol. |
| **Post-market monitoring plan (Art. 72(3))** | Part of the provider's Annex IV documentation; DCP records are inputs to it. |
| **Retention enforcement** | No DCP field or verifier rule enforces a retention period; DCP-08 §3.5 states the principle and the organisation implements it. The `data_disposition` of the decommissioning record (`transferred`, `archived`, `destroyed`) records a decision, it does not enforce one. The `archive_retention_period` shown in the DCP-05 §8.2 example is not a field of `schemas/v2/decommissioning_record.schema.json`. |
| **Actions outside intent gating** | Model inference, interface events and tool calls not declared as intents produce no DCP record. |
| **PQ checkpoint contents (v2.10.0)** | In the v2.10.0 release, no SDK verifier checks PQ checkpoint contents (`spec/VERIFICATION.md` V2-12, [OPERATOR_GUIDE.md](OPERATOR_GUIDE.md)): checkpoints are produced and signed, but a verifier only checks the chain and the Merkle roots. **On `main` the SDK verifiers already check them**; npm SDK 2.2.0 and WASM 2.1.0 include those checks. Other ecosystems have separate releases ([CHANGELOG.md](../CHANGELOG.md), [npm inventory](NPM_RELEASES.md)). |
| **Durable storage in the reference services** | The reference gateway, anchor and transparency log keep their state in memory; the revocation service does too unless `REVOCATION_STATE_PATH` points to persistent storage (an opt-in). The anchor service does not submit on-chain transactions. |
| **Authentication of the reference gateway's DCP-05–09 endpoints** | They validate schemas but do not verify signatures or require a token; anyone who can reach them can change lifecycle, dispute and mandate state ([OPERATOR_GUIDE.md](OPERATOR_GUIDE.md#dcp-0509-service-endpoints)). |
